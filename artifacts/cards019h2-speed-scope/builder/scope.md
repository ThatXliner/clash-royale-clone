# CARDS-019H2 projectile-speed units

## Decision

Keep the unit and conversion of Wizard's pinned projectile `speed: 600` at **QUESTION**. The pinned value is verified as a raw RoyaleAPI field, but no source reviewed here maps it to tiles per second, millitiles per tick, or another physical unit. Do not use it to replace the runtime's 500-millitiles-per-tick estimate.

## Verified source evidence

- **Pinned datamine:** RoyaleAPI `cr-api-data`, commit `54fdef52c5086393b085f57faeeab8ef86b28160` (2023-02-18). In [`docs/json/cards_stats_projectile.json`, lines 998–1039](https://github.com/RoyaleAPI/cr-api-data/blob/54fdef52c5086393b085f57faeeab8ef86b28160/docs/json/cards_stats_projectile.json#L998-L1039), `chr_wizardProjectile` has `speed: 600`, `homing: true`, `radius: 1500`; the entire raw file's recorded SHA-256 is `c883516f54f6c17ee77c15cf484b01da9ded22602214c3b0d42b1ab823bd3197`. The linked Wizard row in [`docs/json/cards_stats_characters.json`, lines 5362–5404](https://github.com/RoyaleAPI/cr-api-data/blob/54fdef52c5086393b085f57faeeab8ef86b28160/docs/json/cards_stats_characters.json#L5362-L5404) has `speed: 60` and `projectile: "chr_wizardProjectile"`; its raw-file SHA-256 is `37734d1508584e43851395c01b2d10046667eb6dc27daef37e5df56e74f933ac`. The JSON values themselves contain no unit annotation.
- **Historical publisher check:** Supercell's 3 May 2019 [May 6th Balance Update notes](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/release-notes/balance-update-coming-5-6/) say “Princess: Projectile Speed increased 33% (450-> 600)” (page lines 31–51). At the pinned 2023 snapshot, `PrincessProjectile` also has `speed: 600` ([same pinned projectile file, lines 1500–1507](https://github.com/RoyaleAPI/cr-api-data/blob/54fdef52c5086393b085f57faeeab8ef86b28160/docs/json/cards_stats_projectile.json#L1500-L1507)). This confirms that Supercell used 600 as a projectile-speed tuning value before the cutoff; the note gives no distance, time, or tick unit.

The pinned `cr-api-data` [README](https://github.com/RoyaleAPI/cr-api-data/blob/54fdef52c5086393b085f57faeeab8ef86b28160/README.md) describes loading its static JSON data and supplies no field-unit definition. The local ledger says raw distances are thousandths of a tile and raw times are milliseconds, then separately maps character movement values 45/60/90/120 to 0.75/1/1.5/2 tiles per second ([`reference/stats/SOURCES.md:25–27`](../../../reference/stats/SOURCES.md#L25); [`reference/stats/SCHEMA.md:31`](../../../reference/stats/SCHEMA.md#L31)). That rule is explicitly for movement; it does not define projectile speed.

The local raw-data extractor lists projectile `speed` among selected fields and hash-checks the pinned files, but performs no speed conversion ([`artifacts/spell-source-supplement/extract_squall_primary_rows.py:16–24,39–58`](../../spell-source-supplement/extract_squall_primary_rows.py#L16)). The prior source analysis states that `600 → 10 tiles/second` is an extrapolation from the character movement scale, not a sourced projectile conversion ([`reference/research/squall-timing-knockback-supplement.md:64–68`](../../../reference/research/squall-timing-knockback-supplement.md#L64)).

## Hypotheses, kept separate from evidence

- **Candidate to test, low evidence:** if projectile `speed` shares the character-movement scale, then `600 / 60 = 10 tiles/second`. The matching field name and the Supercell Princess value make this plausible as a working hypothesis; neither establishes a shared unit or a Wizard conversion.
- **Other scales or a distinct projectile-specific encoding:** remain possible and unranked. No primary source located here supports a competing numeric conversion.
- The current shared profile retains raw `speedRaw: 600` separately from `homingRuntimePolicy.pursuitSpeedMillitilesPerTick: 500` ([`src/shared/ordinary-projectile-delivery-authority.js:28–50,142–162`](../../../src/shared/ordinary-projectile-delivery-authority.js#L28)). That 500 value is a runtime QUESTION policy, not a conversion receipt.

## Bounded next calibration

Resolve the effective speed with one pre-March-2023, frame-calibrated Wizard clip against a stationary target at a known tile distance: identify the projectile's spawn and contact, track its center over frames, and record elapsed frames/ticks. Use projectile spawn as the timing origin; the local ledger defines `first_hit_seconds` as raw `load_time` and cautions that animation, retargeting, projectile travel, and startup can change observed first contact ([`reference/stats/SOURCES.md:27`](../../../reference/stats/SOURCES.md#L27)). This yields an observed tile-per-second rate for the pinned 600 value. A separate moving-target capture is needed to calibrate homing pursuit. If exact conversion from the datamine scalar is required without footage measurement, the remaining decisive evidence is a primary field definition or game-engine source that states the projectile-speed unit and tick conversion.
